# Occasional, useful application checks

A check should determine whether a significant idea can be applied, not whether the learner can repeat the previous sentence. Everything needed must have been explained or explicitly supplied. For each question, identify internally which taught premises support the answer. If there is no such route, teach first.

Prefer a short integrated case: given prior code/range/extremum records and a new execution, determine what novelty is observed, why the input can be retained and what selection does next. A single trace can test several connected ideas. Do not repeatedly test easy arithmetic, immediate recall or the same caveat.

Audit the question before committing: state campaign history and assumptions; ensure one unambiguously best answer in multiple choice; distinguish paper facts, inferences and toy examples. The answer key must be consistent with both the question and the cited source. Do not grade the learner against an unsupported key. When the question or key is flawed, withdraw the assessment and explain the content directly.

Allow 'not taught yet' and 'I do not know' as absence of evidence. Read reasoning independently of the selected option. Sound reasoning with a contradictory click may indicate a selection error. A plausible alternative reading may indicate ambiguity. Neither warrants a chain of remedial quizzes by itself.

Checks are sampling evidence, not a licence to infer durable mastery. Follow the pacing and bounded-repair rules in the teaching fragment.
